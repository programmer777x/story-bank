The no-voice fix

When iOS rejected delayed AI speech, the breakthrough was not another unlock attempt. It was keeping the audio session from going cold.

S

Situation

After LuMi’s temporary phone client became a true host-connected client, speech stopped working in iOS Safari. The old route reached text-to-speech in 1–3 seconds. The new host route needed 5–15 seconds for model thinking and voice generation—long enough for iOS to treat the original tap as stale and reject playback.

T

Task

Restore reliable voice playback on the phone without weakening the host architecture or depending on a user to tap again after every response.

A

Action

After an AI debugging partner cycled through unsuccessful Web Audio fixes, I reframed the problem: iOS did not need to be unlocked later if its audio session never closed. I proposed starting a near-silent looping sound on the original Send gesture and holding it until the real voice arrived.

  • Started a 5-second near-silent WAV loop on Send
  • Stopped the loop as real speech began
  • Moved playback to native audio elements
  • Primed the session with a 20 ms silent WAV
  • Removed the unreliable AudioContext path
  • Kept lip-sync independent of Web Audio
R

Result

LuMi’s voice returned on iOS. The keep-alive was the missing piece, and the approach matched an established production technique known as silent-carrier or keep-alive audio—an answer I reached independently by focusing on the system constraint rather than the failing implementation.

The job-search system

A scattered job hunt became a repeatable operating system: one source of truth, clear file rules, automated intake, complete packets, and follow-ups that do not rely on memory.

S

Situation

I was switching from a decade in call centers into junior Python and remote back-office roles. Resumes, cover letters, and application history were scattered, while leads from my wife’s AI assistant arrived as expiring public links. There was no durable center of gravity.

T

Task

Design a system that could centralize the search and automate the repetitive parts—lead intake, filing, version control, application packets, status tracking, and follow-ups—without giving up human review at the point of submission.

A

Action

I designed a Google Drive architecture around explicit boundaries and a single source of truth. A dedicated job-search inbox receives recruiter and application mail; my wife’s email stays separate, and her assistant feeds leads through one shared roundup document.

  • Tracker sheet owns status, dates, links, and notes
  • Drive zones separate resumes, letters, packets, roundups, and reference
  • Company-Role-YYYYMMDD names every tailored file
  • Each editable document has a matching PDF twin
  • A morning handoff archives leads and queues new roles
  • Every role gets a standardized, review-ready packet
  • I review and submit applications with legal attestations
  • Follow-up checks are tied to tracker dates
R

Result

The system moved from zero structure to an operating pipeline in days. Every packet is versioned, each status is traceable, and new leads flow into an archive and review queue automatically.

The gatekeeper bot

A familiar community problem became a working Python automation—and a public example of how to build, deploy, monitor, and grow a useful product.

S

Situation

I was returning to programming after years away and wanted to build a business around practical automation. Rather than choose an unfamiliar problem just to practice Python, I started with Discord—a platform I already knew well as a gamer and longtime user.

T

Task

Create a small but useful Discord product that could automate access and rewards inside a community, then keep it available on free-tier infrastructure without turning deployment and monitoring into a second project.

A

Action

I built a lightweight gatekeeper bot in Python with discord.py. It watches a designated channel, checks each response against a secret answer, grants the winner a specified reward role, and deletes the winning answer so the challenge is not spoiled for everyone else.

  • Turned a community ritual into an event-driven workflow
  • Automated role assignment after a correct response
  • Removed winning answers to protect the challenge
  • Hosted the project on Render’s free web tier
  • Used Flask alongside a background ping worker
  • Added UptimeRobot for external availability monitoring
R

Result

The project became a running, monitored automation with room to expand into tools for streamers, gaming tournaments, and membership-based servers. UptimeRobot selected the work for its Community Spotlight, profiling both the bot and the learning process behind deploying and monitoring it.
